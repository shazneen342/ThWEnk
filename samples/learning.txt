Host: Today we are discussing how to learn from podcasts instead of only listening passively. A listener may recognise a term without being able to explain it. Recognition and understanding are different.

Guest: One useful practice is to pause after a short section and explain its main idea in your own words. If you cannot explain a term, write it down and revisit the relevant passage. For example, after hearing about opportunity cost, you could explain it as the value of the next-best option you give up when making a choice.

Host: Imagine a student who can spend an hour studying or working at a shop. If the student chooses to study, the wages they could have earned are one part of the opportunity cost. This example does not prove that studying is the wrong choice. The student may value future learning more than today's income.

Guest: Summaries also involve choices. A very short summary can leave out uncertainty or disagreement. A helpful companion should let readers inspect the original passage. A speaker's confident statement is not automatically strong evidence.

Host: Language matters too. An explanation in a familiar language can make an unfamiliar concept easier to discuss. However, a translation can change meaning. Technical words may need an explanation and the original term side by side.

Guest: We have described practical suggestions, not reported an experiment comparing students. We cannot conclude from this conversation how much these methods improve exam scores. A future study could compare comprehension with and without a companion while accounting for prior knowledge and language proficiency.
